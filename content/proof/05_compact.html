The shapes $S_{g}^{\pm1}$ are thin: three cells wide and about $6g$ cells long. Nothing in Theorem 1 asks for that. A simple sieve produces admissible sets of any outline, full of holes.

Definition. Let $P\ge2$ and choose a class $a_p\in(\mathbb Z/p)^{2}$ for every prime $p\le P$. The sieved set is

$$V(P,(a_p))=\{v\in\mathbb Z^{2}:\ v\not\equiv a_p\pmod p\ \text{ for every prime }p\le P\}.$$

It is periodic modulo $\prod_{p\le P}p$ in both coordinates, and by the Chinese remainder theorem its density is $\prod_{p\le P}\left(1-p^{-2}\right)$, which decreases to $6/\pi^{2}=0.6079\ldots$ as $P$ grows.

Lemma 10 (sieved sets are admissible). Let $S$ be a finite subset of a sieved set $V(P,(a_p))$ that lies in at most $P$ consecutive columns. Then $S$ is admissible.

Proof. For a prime $p\le P$ the set $S$ misses the class $a_p$. For a prime $p>P$ the column indices of $S$ take at most $P\lt p$ values, so some residue $a$ modulo $p$ is not the column index of any cell of $S$, and $S$ misses every class $(a,b)$. $\blacksquare$

Corollary 11. Every finite connected subset $S$ of a sieved set $V(P,(a_p))$ lying in at most $P$ consecutive columns is a region of region type $+1$ for infinitely many $(x,d)$ and a region of region type $-1$ for infinitely many $(x,d)$, with the genus of $S$. The same holds with rows in place of columns.

Proof. Lemma 10 and Corollary 2(a). With rows in place of columns the proof of Lemma 10 is the same, with the row indices. $\blacksquare$
